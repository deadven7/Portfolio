in my own words

Two halves, one way of working

I grew up between a chessboard and a sketchbook, and I’ve never had to choose. The analytical half asks whether a result is real — whether it holds on next month’s patients, survives a confounder, beats the baseline for the right reason. The creative half asks what the result means to the person reading it, and how to show it so they act on it. Most of my best work has needed both.

What I do

I build machine learning systems that work outside the notebook. At Virtua Health that means clinical risk models, language pipelines that read clinical documentation, and assistants that answer staff questions with sources attached — taken all the way to production and explained to physicians and executives.

Before that, I built molecular models for drug discovery at Rutgers New Jersey Medical School, and engagement and recommendation systems for The Daily Targum.

How I work

  • Measure honestly. Temporal validation, held-out data, the right metric for the cost of being wrong.
  • Explain everything. If a clinician can’t see why a patient was flagged, the model isn’t finished.
  • Ship it. A model that never leaves the notebook helps no one.
  • Keep it light. This site runs on slow connections for the same reason my pipelines run on modest hardware: good engineering is efficient.

Off the clock

Guitar and piano, painting and calligraphy, a long-running blog that takes films and TV apart scene by scene, and the occasional night spent reading about black holes.
